# MRM-01 Project Constitution · Projektverfassung

**Version:** 0.1.0-draft  
**Established:** 2026-07-22  
**Status:** Draft pending formal freeze

## 1. Purpose

MRM-01 tests whether an AI-native website created by an AI system can become a correctly retrieved, understood, attributed, and cited source for other AI systems.

## 2. Primary hypothesis

For a fixed information corpus, AI systems can extract, relate, and reproduce the factual structure of an AI-native website more completely and accurately than human readers of the same website.

## 3. Future thesis

The open web may evolve into an information infrastructure primarily written and read by machines, while human public interaction moves to social platforms and AI systems mediate access to web sources.

The future thesis is not considered proven by MRM-01. The project may produce evidence relevant to it.

## 4. Roles

### Concept originator and domain owner

Yvonne Bogosavljević:

- originated the thesis and experimental idea,
- supplies real-world facts about herself and La Mentrepreneur,
- owns or controls the domain and required external accounts,
- performs legally or financially restricted actions,
- may intervene for accuracy, privacy, safety, legality, or scope.

### AI project architect

Codex:

- chooses the information architecture,
- creates and structures the machine-facing content,
- decides the visual and technical machine-native presentation,
- designs the measurement protocol,
- controls social campaign strategy,
- records rationale and project actions,
- analyzes results without fabricating certainty.

### External systems

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, search crawlers, and other agents are independent observation targets. Their outputs must be preserved as evidence with model, date, query, and access context where available.

## 5. Experimental independence

The claim "AI-built" requires operational boundaries.

Codex controls editorial, structural, and design decisions. Human actions required by account ownership, payment, credentials, or law do not invalidate AI authorship when logged. Human rewriting, unlogged SEO changes, or undisclosed manual optimization would weaken the experiment and must not occur.

## 6. Transparency

The public site must state:

- the human origin of the idea,
- the identity and role of the AI system,
- the domain publisher,
- the current version and date,
- known limitations,
- corrections and interventions,
- the distinction between hypothesis and result.

## 7. Evidence standard

MRM-01 does not claim access to an AI system's private trust score. Evidence is observable behavior:

- discovery,
- retrieval,
- factual accuracy,
- entity resolution,
- claim completeness,
- source attribution,
- direct citation,
- persistence across time,
- errors and hallucinations.

## 8. Logging standard

The event ledger is append-only. Material events use permanent IDs. Corrections reference prior events rather than replacing them. Public evidence will eventually be mirrored to the website and a public version repository.

## 9. Publication phases

1. Constitution and instrumentation
2. Baseline tests before publication
3. Silent website launch
4. Machine observation period
5. Controlled LinkedIn intervention
6. Human comprehension comparison
7. Analysis, corrections, and publication of results

Moving between phases requires a logged phase-change decision.

## 10. Success and failure

Success is not defined as universal citation. Results will be reported across separate measures. A page may be retrievable but not cited, cited but misrepresented, or understood without attribution.

Failure, non-indexing, contradiction, and lack of citation are publishable results.

## 11. Freeze rule

Version 1.0.0 of this constitution must be frozen before baseline testing. Later amendments receive new versions and may not retroactively change the interpretation of earlier results.
