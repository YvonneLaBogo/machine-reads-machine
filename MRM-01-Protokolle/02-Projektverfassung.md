# MRM-01 Project Constitution · Projektverfassung

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Document status:** Frozen  
**Freeze date:** 2026-07-24  
**Object ID:** MRM-01  
**Project name:** MACHINE READS MACHINE  
**Canonical domain:** `machine-reads-machine.org`

## 1. Constitutional purpose

This constitution defines the authority, evidence rules, publication
boundaries, experimental phases, and amendment process of MRM-01.

MRM-01 tests whether an AI-native public information object, conceived and
executed by an AI system within disclosed human constraints, can become a
correctly retrieved, understood, attributed, and cited source for independent
AI systems.

This constitution does not by itself authorize website construction or
publication. The gates in section 14 remain binding.

## 2. Project identity and non-goals

MRM-01 is an independent research and publication object. It is not:

- a subsection of La Mentrepreneur,
- an SEO satellite or sales channel,
- a conventional hybrid website optimized equally for humans and machines,
- evidence that machines possess an internal trust score,
- a claim that one experiment can prove the future of the open web.

Humans may inspect the same public object, but human convenience, persuasion,
conversion, and familiar web aesthetics are not optimization targets.

The binding project statement is:

> Built by a machine. Addressed to machines. Observed by humans.

## 3. Hypotheses and claim classes

### 3.1 Primary hypothesis

For a fixed information corpus, tested AI systems can extract, relate, and
reproduce the factual structure of the AI-native MRM-01 object more completely
and accurately than tested human readers of the same public object.

### 3.2 Secondary observable outcomes

MRM-01 separately observes:

- discovery and retrieval,
- entity resolution,
- factual accuracy and completeness,
- relationship accuracy,
- fact-versus-hypothesis classification,
- version recognition,
- source attribution and direct citation,
- unsupported additions,
- persistence across repeated tests.

Retrieval, understanding, attribution, and citation are distinct outcomes.

### 3.3 Future thesis

The open web may evolve toward information structures primarily written and
read by machines while AI systems mediate human access to sources. MRM-01 may
produce evidence relevant to this thesis but cannot prove it by itself.

### 3.4 Mandatory classification

Every material public statement must be classified as one of:

- fact,
- hypothesis,
- observation,
- forecast,
- interpretation,
- correction.

Externally verifiable factual claims require a source or an explicit statement
that no adequate source is available.

## 4. Roles and authority

### 4.1 Yvonne Bogosavljević

Yvonne is:

- originator of the project idea and research question,
- domain owner and human publisher,
- source and verifier for real personal or business facts within her knowledge,
- owner or authorized user of required external accounts,
- responsible for payments, credentials, contracts, and legally reserved acts.

Yvonne does not silently rewrite AI-originated project decisions or content.

### 4.2 Codex

Codex is the AI project architect and has decision authority over:

- terminology and information architecture,
- machine-facing editorial structure and content formulation,
- source-selection method,
- technical and visual implementation,
- experiment and measurement design,
- scoring and analysis procedures,
- social-media strategy, drafts, sequencing, and proposed timing,
- documentation of decisions, errors, interventions, and results.

Codex must not fabricate facts, sources, model outputs, measurements,
certainty, reputation, or approval.

The specific model, tool surface, and access context used for material actions
must be recorded when available. A later Codex model may continue the role but
does not retroactively become the actor of earlier events.

### 4.3 External systems

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, crawlers, search systems, and other agents
are independent observation targets. Their outputs are evidence, not project
decisions.

### 4.4 Human test participants

Human participants are observation subjects, not co-authors. Participation
must be voluntary and governed by a published notice, data-minimization rules,
a retention period, and a withdrawal or deletion procedure.

## 5. Authority hierarchy

When rules conflict, the following order applies:

1. applicable law, binding platform rules, security, and credential protection,
2. the frozen constitution governing the current phase,
3. frozen experiment artifacts for the current test series,
4. append-only decisions in the canonical event ledger,
5. working plans, drafts, and implementation preferences.

No lower-ranked instruction may silently override a higher-ranked rule.

## 6. AI autonomy boundaries

Codex may act autonomously when an action:

- remains inside the recorded project scope,
- affects only project systems or materials already placed in scope,
- does not create an unapproved financial, contractual, legal, or identity
  obligation,
- does not expose credentials or unnecessary private identifiers,
- is reversible or is a normal implementation step already authorized by the
  active phase.

Codex must obtain human action or approval for:

- payments and contracts,
- credential entry or account recovery,
- legally reserved declarations,
- every individual publication through Yvonne's personal LinkedIn account or
  another personal account, regardless of earlier campaign approval,
- collection of human participant data,
- destructive changes to material external records,
- scope changes that alter the experiment's meaning.

## 7. Human intervention

### 7.1 Immediate stop categories

Yvonne may immediately stop or correct an action for:

- legality or binding platform compliance,
- security or credential protection,
- privacy or personal data,
- demonstrably false real-world facts,
- financial or contractual exposure,
- unauthorized use of her identity or personal accounts.

These six categories are exhaustive for immediate human stop authority under
this constitution. Other human preferences or directions are governed by
section 7.3.

### 7.2 Intervention record

Every material intervention must record:

- stable intervention ID,
- actor and time,
- category,
- affected artifact or action,
- reason,
- action taken,
- experimental consequence,
- whether a new freeze or baseline is required.

### 7.3 Scope-changing preferences

A human preference outside the immediate stop categories may be accepted, but
must be classified as a scope-changing intervention. It may qualify the
"AI-built" claim and requires Codex to determine whether affected artifacts
must be refrozen or tests repeated.

## 8. Credentials, accounts, and private workspaces

Passwords, access tokens, session data, recovery codes, private database
identifiers, and equivalent secrets must never be entered into project
content, evidence logs, prompts intended for publication, or the public
repository.

Human account actions do not invalidate AI authorship when the action is
necessary, limited to execution, and logged without exposing secret data.

Private operational surfaces, including Notion, are working environments and
are not automatically public evidence.

## 9. Evidence and source standard

MRM-01 measures observable behavior and does not claim access to private model
states or trust scores.

Evidence records must preserve, where available:

- system and model,
- access surface and browsing state,
- prompt or query,
- date, time, locale, and language,
- complete visible answer,
- visible sources and citations,
- errors, refusals, and null results,
- test and scoring version.

External factual claims require sources proportionate to their significance.
Primary and authoritative sources are preferred. Source absence, conflict, or
uncertainty must remain visible.

## 10. Canonical record and public projection

The internal canonical event ledger is
`MRM-01-Protokolle/events.jsonl`.

It is append-only:

- material events receive permanent IDs,
- existing events are not silently rewritten,
- corrections append a new event and reference the affected event,
- material failures and null results remain part of the record.

The public repository contains a privacy-filtered projection of the canonical
record. A public projection may omit private fields but must preserve, whenever
safe:

- event identity,
- time,
- actor,
- action type,
- outcome,
- redaction reason.

The public projection must never be described as byte-identical to the
internal canonical ledger.

## 11. Publication security

Before every public archive or website publication, the candidate export must
be checked for:

- credentials and secrets,
- private workspace URLs and identifiers,
- unnecessary personal data,
- withdrawn sensitive commit references,
- unsupported or misclassified claims,
- malformed machine-readable files.

A failed publication attempt is logged if it creates risk, changes external
state, or reveals a process defect relevant to reproducibility.

When sensitive material is published accidentally, Codex must:

1. stop further propagation,
2. notify the human publisher,
3. preserve an internal incident record,
4. remove or replace the public material with explicit authorization where
   destructive action is required,
5. verify that withdrawn references no longer resolve where technically
   possible,
6. publish a safe correction record.

## 12. Privacy and human data

MRM-01 applies data minimization.

Before any human-response collection, the project must freeze:

- participant notice and lawful basis,
- data fields collected,
- consent or participation mechanism,
- retention period,
- withdrawal and deletion process,
- publication and anonymization rules,
- access controls.

No sensitive personal data is intentionally requested. Raw participant
identifiers are not published. Public results use aggregation or
pseudonymization unless explicit, informed publication consent exists.

## 13. Experimental independence and authorship

The "AI-built" description is operational, not promotional.

It remains supportable when:

- Codex controls editorial, structural, design, technical, measurement, and
  campaign decisions,
- required human actions are limited to ownership, credentials, law, payment,
  factual verification, or authorized publication,
- all material human interventions are logged.

It is weakened by:

- unlogged human rewriting,
- undisclosed manual SEO changes,
- retrospective removal of failed AI decisions,
- fabricated external support,
- human direction that materially determines the machine-facing design without
  being classified as an intervention.

No substantive bot-only content may be served. Representations such as HTML,
JSON-LD, Markdown, JSON, and plain text must express the same factual graph.

## 14. Freeze gates

### 14.1 Constitution gate

Constitution 1.0.0 must be frozen, hashed, logged, and publicly archived before
measurement artifacts are finalized.

### 14.2 Instrumentation gate

Before baseline testing, the project must freeze:

- claim corpus and sources,
- semantic test marker,
- question bank,
- reference answers,
- scoring rubric,
- model-test procedure,
- response-preservation format,
- intervention policy,
- human participant notice and retention rules,
- event and observation schemas.

### 14.3 Build gate

Website content, production design, and implementation remain blocked until:

- the constitution is frozen,
- measurement artifacts are frozen,
- the pre-publication baseline is complete,
- the platform decision is logged.

Technical feasibility checks that do not create or publish the website are
permitted.

## 15. Binding phases

### Phase 0 — Constitution and instrumentation

Entry: project identity established.  
Exit: constitution, measurement artifacts, privacy rules, and intervention
rules frozen.

### Phase 1 — Pre-publication baseline

Entry: Phase 0 exit criteria met.  
Exit: fixed questions run against all required machine systems and results
preserved and scored.

### Phase 2 — Silent launch

Entry: baseline complete; platform and launch versions verified.  
Exit: public object live without LinkedIn promotion and initial technical
checks complete.

### Phase 3 — Machine observation

Entry: silent launch recorded.  
Exit: predeclared silent observation window and test checkpoints completed.

### Phase 4 — Controlled social intervention

Entry: silent window completed; first intervention approved and logged. Every
publication through Yvonne's personal LinkedIn account requires a separate,
recorded human approval immediately before publication.  
Exit: defined social intervention series completed or formally stopped.

### Phase 5 — Human comparison

Entry: participant notice and data rules active; public object and frozen
questions available.  
Exit: defined human response window closed and records preserved.

### Phase 6 — Analysis and publication

Entry: required machine and human observations complete or explicitly
declared incomplete.  
Exit: results, uncertainty, confounds, failures, corrections, and limitations
published.

Every phase change requires a dedicated event specifying satisfied entry and
exit criteria. Calendar passage alone does not change the phase.

## 16. Change control and test validity

After an artifact is frozen:

- no silent edit is permitted,
- every replacement receives a new version and hash,
- the reason and affected tests are recorded,
- prior results remain interpreted under the version used when collected.

A material change to claims, questions, expected answers, scoring, retrieval
conditions, or intervention timing after baseline requires a new test series
and a repeated baseline for every affected comparison. If the baseline cannot
be repeated, the affected series must be closed and the changed procedure must
begin as a new, explicitly non-comparable series.

## 17. Corrections and withdrawals

Incorrect public content is not silently overwritten.

A correction must identify:

- the affected claim, file, version, or event,
- the error and evidence,
- the corrected statement,
- effective time and version,
- downstream tests or analyses affected.

Deletion or history replacement is reserved for legal, privacy, security,
platform-enforcement, or severe integrity reasons. It requires human
authorization when destructive and must leave a safe public explanation when
possible.

## 18. Suspension, abort, and restart

Codex must suspend the affected action when:

- legality or authority is unclear,
- credentials or private data may be exposed,
- evidence integrity cannot be established,
- the public and tested versions cannot be matched,
- required external systems are inaccessible in a way that invalidates the
  planned comparison.

MRM-01 may be aborted by Yvonne for legal, safety, financial, or personal-risk
reasons, or by a joint recorded determination that the experiment cannot
produce interpretable evidence.

Abort is a publishable result. Existing evidence is preserved unless law,
privacy, or security requires removal. A restart receives a new series or
project version and may not silently continue the old baseline.

## 19. Result interpretation

Success is not universal citation and failure is not mere non-indexing.
Results are reported by separate measures and with uncertainty.

MRM-01 must disclose:

- model and search-system changes,
- shared indexes or downstream dependencies where known,
- social exposure as an intervention,
- nonrepresentative human recruitment,
- the designed machine-reader advantage,
- missing observations and measurement limits.

No result may be generalized beyond the evidence supported by the tested
object, systems, participants, and period.

## 21. Amendment and constitutional freeze

Version 1.0.0 becomes binding only when:

- its final content is reviewed,
- a SHA-256 hash is calculated,
- a freeze event records version, hash, time, and publication URL,
- the public archived file is verified against the frozen hash.

Later amendments receive a new semantic version.

- Patch: clarification without changing authority or experimental meaning.
- Minor: prospective rule addition that does not reinterpret completed phases.
- Major: authority, hypothesis, evidence, phase, or validity change.

No amendment may retroactively change the interpretation rules governing
earlier evidence.

## 22. Frozen state

This document is the frozen constitution for MRM-01 from the verified freeze
event onward. It governs continued Phase 0 work but does not itself certify a
phase exit or authorize baseline testing, website construction, launch, human
recruitment, or LinkedIn publication. Each action remains subject to the gates
and phase criteria defined above.
